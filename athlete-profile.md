## Strength / gym
Actual gym frequency is **3x/week** (corrected 2026-07-15 — earlier assumed
5x/week from his initial description, which was wrong). Priority is to
**not lose any muscle** while building endurance volume — not to maximize
hypertrophy. Also wants to work in plyometrics/impact-tolerance work and
recovery/biometric check-ins, which are new territory for him.

**Previous split** (his own description, upper-body only, no dedicated leg
day): Push, Pull, Rest, Chest/Back, Shoulders/Arms, Rest. He flagged himself
that he wasn't sure this was optimal and asked for a redesign.

**Current split (2026-07-15) — Anterior/Posterior/Upper (A/P/U), 3
sessions/week, one of each, no repeats:**
- **Anterior** (squats, lunges, step-ups, ankle/landing mechanics, low-level
  plyometric progression) — quad-dominant, the most impact-sensitive
  session. The previous split had *no* dedicated leg day at all. Start plyo
  conservatively at 1x/week since it's new territory for him, progressing
  gradually given the ankle history.
- **Posterior** (RDLs, hip thrusts, glute bridges, hamstring curls) —
  hip-hinge/posterior-chain work, protective for runners and feeds hip
  drive for both running and cycling. Still leg-dominant so keep off hard
  run days, but lower impact-shock than the Anterior/plyo session.
- **Upper** (combined push + pull — chest/shoulders/triceps + back/biceps
  in one session) — lowest interference with endurance work, most flexible
  placement, and the natural session to pair with a pool-gym swim day (see
  swim/pool-gym note above).
- 4 rest/endurance-only days — used for light recovery check-ins (resting
  HR on waking, soreness/RPE log, sleep quality) rather than adding a
  separate session.

**Sequencing rule, most to least impact-sensitive**: Anterior+plyo >
Posterior > Upper. Keep the Anterior/plyo day furthest from hard run/
long-run days; Posterior can sit a bit closer if needed; Upper interferes
least and has the most scheduling freedom (including pairing with swim).

At only 3 sessions/week each muscle group/pattern is hit ~1x/week — a
maintenance dose, not a growth dose, which is the right trade-off given
Hamburg is the primary goal: lower gym frequency also reduces the risk of
concurrent-training interference with endurance adaptations at this
combined volume (8-13+ hrs/week endurance).

## Coaching philosophy

_Built 2026-07-15 via researched coach study, a 7-question interview, and a
6-month Strava analysis. This section is the "how" behind every decision in
this file and in `training/plan.md` / `plan-my-week`._

### What I'm training for, exactly
- **Primary goal: Ironman Hamburg (full distance), 2027-06-06, target
  sub-10:00:00.** This is the real priority — triathlon is the main focus.
- **Important secondary goal: Munich Marathon, 2026-10-11, target
  sub-2:48:00.** Genuinely wants to run this well, not just survive it, but
  not at the cost of swim/bike fitness.
- **Why it matters**, in his own words: "It means everything... this is my
  life now. It's building my personality, shaping my personality... I'm
  dedicated." This isn't a hobby goal — treat lapses in commitment as out of
  character, not the baseline to expect.
- **These targets are living, not fixed** — see below.

### The influences

Researched three coaches/schools chosen to match a self-described
competitive-and-aggressive-but-science-driven style: **Brett Sutton**
(triathlon, authoritarian, toughness-first, skeptical of gadgets — coached
Chrissie Wellington and Nicola Spirig), **Joe Friel** (triathlon/endurance,
Bompa-derived periodization, TSS/data-anchored, structured recovery), and
**Renato Canova** (marathon-specific, race-pace-precise "special block"
training, minimal emphasis on pure base volume). Full research writeup
with citations is in the conversation this was built from; the syntheses
below are what actually got used.

### The core identity: Sutton-rooted, deliberately

The interview surfaced a clear pattern: "I bounce back brutal... once I hit
a wall, I want to break that wall, not stop at that wall." That's Sutton's
whole philosophy — psychology-first, toughness forged through genuinely hard
sessions, no cushioning when things go wrong ("direct and blunt" was the
explicit answer on how to be talked to when struggling). **This is the
emotional core of the philosophy**: sessions should be allowed to be
genuinely brutal, not comfortably-hard, and feedback on missed standards
should be direct, not softened.

### The guardrail the data demands: Friel + Canova-rooted, non-negotiable

The 6-month Strava analysis complicates the self-report in one specific way:
the "bounce back brutal" instinct, in practice, produced three
week-over-week volume spikes (+45%, +134%, **+484%**) — not resilience,
but the absence of a governor. Every spike followed an artificially low
week: a pattern of compressed recovery, not planned recovery, that wasn't
consciously visible until the data was pulled.

**This is why the philosophy is not pure Sutton.** The wall-breaking
instinct is real and should be used, not suppressed — but it gets *channeled
into the sessions themselves* (via Canova's precision: race-pace-anchored,
quantified hard work, not vague "go hard") and *bounded by Friel's structural
recovery* (mandatory down-week every 3-4 build weeks; ~10% weekly volume
ceiling checked against the trailing 4-week average, not just the prior
week — see `plan-my-week`). The guardrail exists **because** of who he is,
not despite it: real toughness here means following the recovery protocol
even when he feels like he could go harder, because that's what keeps him
breaking walls next month instead of getting hurt.

**Concrete non-negotiable arising directly from this:** warm-up/mobility
before every hard or long session is mandatory, not optional. He identified
himself that skipping it is when his previously-broken left leg flares up
"in different places" — and the moments he's most likely to skip it (rushed,
making up for lost time, mid-spike) are exactly the moments the data shows
he's most at risk. This rule exists specifically to let the aggressive
instinct operate safely, not to blunt it.

### Data AND feel, not data vs. feel

Sutton and Friel genuinely disagree here — Sutton is skeptical of gadgets,
Friel is metrics-anchored. His own answer split the difference on purpose:
"HR zones and base power and also feel and RPE are both important... they
go hand in hand." **Application**: every key session gets both a hard
target (HR zone / pace / power, from the zones in this file) and a feel/RPE
check. When they diverge — HR creeping up at an easy pace, RPE feeling much
harder than the zone suggests — that's treated as a fatigue signal worth
investigating, not noise to ignore.

### Living targets, reviewed at checkpoints, not fixed for a year

When asked about season-level structure, the answer was explicit: re-adjust
based on real response, because the goal itself might shift — "maybe now
the goal shifts, not sub-10, but sub-9:30... we take it one session at a
time." This is closer to Friel's "recovery on demand" adaptiveness than a
fixed roadmap, and it directly informs how `training/plan.md` should be
used: **the sub-2:48 / sub-10:00 targets above are the current
best-evidence goals, not locked-in promises.** Revisit them explicitly at
defined checkpoints — after Munich, and at major milestones through the
Ironman build — based on what the data actually shows by then, not
optimism or pessimism in the moment.
