# Race Dossier — Munich Marathon, 2026-10-11

_Built 2026-07-15. Sources cited throughout; uncertain points flagged
explicitly rather than guessed._

## 1. The course

**Format**: single-lap loop, ~28,500 total participants across all race
distances (marathon field ~6,000), starting on Ludwigstrasse near the
Siegestor arch, through Schwabing, past BMW Welt and the Olympiaturm,
through the Werksviertel quarter, south via Giesing into the city center,
the Marienplatz highlight around km 30-35, then Sendlinger Tor and
Theresienwiese (the Oktoberfest grounds) before finishing inside the
Olympiapark — the last stretch runs through a tunnel into the Olympic
Stadium.
[RunDida race guide](https://rundida.com/marathon/munich/) ·
[Watch Athletics course map](https://www.watchathletics.com/page/7056/marathon-m-nchen-course-map)

**Elevation**: sources vary slightly — 90m, 96m, and 100m total gain are
the most commonly cited figures; one outlier source says 185m (likely
counting gain+loss together rather than gain alone — flagged as uncertain).
Elevation ranges roughly 498-532m. **This is a flat course** — Statathon
rates it "Moderate difficulty," harder than only 20% of marathons
worldwide, ranked #917 of 1,150 analyzed and #28 of 35 in Germany.
[Statathon elevation data](https://statathon.com/munich-marathon/)

**Climbs**: concentrated in the second half. Multiple independent finisher
reports describe real difficulty from ~km 32 through the finish, worst
around km 37-40 — one described legs "like two Belgian waffles" by 40km
with IT band pain setting in from ~37km. One report also mentions a hill
around km 17 that "demanded more energy than expected" while running
fresh — **this is a single-source detail I can't fully corroborate against
the official ~90-100m-total-gain figures, so treat it as a minor undulation
that felt harder than expected, not a major climb.**
[theathleteblog race report](https://theathleteblog.com/munich-marathon-my-first-marathon/)

**Surface**: 100% asphalt/road, World Athletics certified. No technical
sections.
[Statathon](https://statathon.com/munich-marathon/)

**Altitude**: Munich sits at ~520m above sea level (498-532m across the
course) — well below the ~1,500m+ threshold where altitude meaningfully
affects endurance performance. **Not a factor, unless you train somewhere
notably different in elevation — I don't have your home training elevation
on file, flag it if relevant.**
[Elevation-check.com](https://elevation-check.com/cities/en/munich.html)

## 2. Conditions

**Historical weather**: October in Munich averages 8-14°C, humidity
estimates range 65-85% depending on source, wind ~11km/h, rain chance
cited anywhere from 25-40% (rarely heavy) with ~65-77mm average monthly
rainfall across ~12 rain days. Race organizers and multiple sources call
this "near-ideal" marathon weather.
[RunDida](https://rundida.com/marathon/munich/) ·
[Weather averages](https://www.holiday-weather.com/munich/averages/october/)

**What it's actually like on race morning**: colder than the averages
suggest at the 10:00 AM start — one finisher described "a few degrees
above freezing," another logged 11°C and still overdressed, warning that
"once you start running it will feel 20°F warmer" than it does standing in
the corral.
[Marathon Training Academy](https://www.marathontrainingacademy.com/munich-marathon) ·
[theathleteblog](https://theathleteblog.com/munich-marathon-my-first-marathon/)

## 3. What finishers warn about

**The #1 warning, repeated independently across multiple reports: the flat
course feels deceptively easy for the first half, runners go out too fast,
and the second half falls apart.** One finisher's second half was 17
minutes slower than the first; another hit heart-rate Zone 4 in the first
5km and had nothing left for the final 10km. Direct quote: "I didn't have
any experience of racing 2+ hours, so didn't know that feeling good at
halfway doesn't mean anything."
[theathleteblog](https://theathleteblog.com/munich-marathon-my-first-marathon/) ·
[Chris Eidhof race report](https://chris.eidhof.nl/post/race-report-munich-marathon/)

**Fueling**: standard aid-station gel timing (14km and 28km) was called
"insufficient" by at least one finisher, who needed more nutrition earlier
than the course provides by default. Aid stations otherwise run every
2.5-4km with water/isotonic alternating, gels at 14/28km, bananas and bars
from ~28km onward.
[theathleteblog](https://theathleteblog.com/munich-marathon-my-first-marathon/) ·
[Marathon Training Academy](https://www.marathontrainingacademy.com/munich-marathon)

**Crowd support drops off exactly when it hurts most**: Marienplatz
(~30-35km) is "amazing," but the final 4-7km through Olympiapark is
comparatively lonely — right where the km 37-40 difficulty concentrates.
[theathleteblog](https://theathleteblog.com/munich-marathon-my-first-marathon/) ·
[Racecheck reviews](https://racecheck.com/races/munich-marathon/)

**One finisher's blunt advice**: never walk if you can avoid it — resuming
running after walking is exponentially harder than holding a slower run.

## 4. Gear & logistics

- **Start**: first wave 10:00 AM at Ludwigstrasse/Siegestor (U-Bahn
  Universität station), corral-based by pace (e.g., a mid-pack corral
  started ~10:20 AM in one account) — arrive with real buffer, not "just
  in time."
- **Finish**: Olympiapark, U3 to Olympiazentrum.
- **Transit**: free U-Bahn access on race day with your bib.
- **Registration**: ~€89, first-come-first-served (no lottery), currently
  open at marathonmuenchen.org. Time limit 6h30m (9:15/km pace) — not a
  factor at your goal pace.
- **Expo/packet pickup**: exists pre-race with vendor booths, but exact
  2026 venue/hours weren't confirmed in the sources checked — **flagged as
  uncertain, confirm closer to race day via the official site.**
- **Gear recommendation given the cold-but-warms-fast start**: a
  disposable throwaway layer (poncho or old long sleeve) for the corral,
  race kit underneath tuned for ~10°C effective feel once moving, not the
  standing-around temperature.
[Marathon Training Academy](https://www.marathontrainingacademy.com/munich-marathon) ·
[RunDida](https://rundida.com/marathon/munich/)

## 5. Against your actual fitness — honest read

**Correction first**: earlier analysis wrongly assumed you'd never raced a
marathon. You have — **2:58:00, ~8 months ago (~Nov 2025)**, alongside a
1:27:00 half marathon around the same time, both before your connected
Strava history starts. Munich isn't your first marathon, it's a return to
the distance chasing a ~10-minute PR off a much larger, more diverse
training base than you had then.

**Does your base support 3:59/km for 42km — your stated biggest worry?**
Target pace for sub-2:48:00 is almost exactly 3:59/km. Using your recent 5K
(17:39, i.e. 3:32/km) and the Riegel race-prediction formula (T2 = T1 ×
(D2/D1)^1.06, a standard and long-established endurance-prediction method),
your current 5K speed predicts a **~2:49:18 marathon** — 1-2 minutes slower
than your A goal, sitting almost exactly between your A and B goals. That's
a genuinely encouraging number. **But Riegel predicts what your engine can
do, not what your legs can survive** — it assumes marathon-adequate
long-run durability and fueling execution, which is exactly the open
question here: your longest run within visible Strava history is 21.1km,
half the race distance, and there's no rehearsed fueling plan on record yet.

**So, specifically:**
- **Strength**: raw aerobic speed is not the limiter. 5K/1K times support
  the goal comfortably.
- **Exposure #1**: long-run durability past 21km is unproven in current
  training-cycle data — this is exactly where the km 37-40 course
  difficulty and your pace-sustainability worry both land.
- **Exposure #2**: your own instinct to push hard and not back off (from
  your coaching philosophy) is a real liability on *this specific course*,
  given how consistently finishers report blowing up from an
  easy-feeling-but-too-fast first half.
- **Exposure #3**: no tested race-day fueling strategy, and finisher
  reports say the default aid-station timing isn't enough.
- **Uncertain**: your home training elevation relative to Munich's ~520m —
  flag if materially different.

## What this means I need to train

1. **Push long runs from 21.1km toward 28-32km**, with the final 8-10km of
   at least one long run at goal marathon pace (~3:59/km) — rehearses
   sustaining target pace specifically while fatigued, directly targeting
   your stated worry.
2. **Marathon-pace-anchored specific-endurance sessions** (already the
   Canova-influenced approach in `training/plan.md`), not generic tempo —
   teaches the body that 3:59/km feels moderate by surrounding it with
   similar efforts.
3. **Rehearse race-day fueling on long runs** — test carb intake earlier
   and more frequently than the 14km/28km default, since finishers flagged
   that spacing as insufficient. Find what works before race day, not on it.
4. **Deliberately practice conservative/even pacing**, given your
   wall-breaking instinct is exactly the failure mode multiple finishers
   describe. Consider a slightly negative-split strategy as the default
   race plan.
5. **Warm-up/mobility stays non-negotiable**, especially before long runs —
   your own ankle-flare trigger and the late-race breakdown pattern
   finishers describe (IT band, "legs stop bending") point at the same
   underlying risk: accumulated fatigue without preparation.
6. **Test a throwaway-layer strategy** in a cold training session — race
   morning is colder than the averages suggest.
