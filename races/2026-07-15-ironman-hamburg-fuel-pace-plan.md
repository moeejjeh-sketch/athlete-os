## Why this whole plan is a step down from the Munich numbers

Your 90g/hr carb tolerance is real, but it was only proven for a ~2-3 hour
window. This race keeps you fueling for **9-11+ hours**. Sustained
high-carb intake gets harder to hold as duration stretches — and the
Hamburg dossier already has direct evidence of this: a real finisher
reported gels turning "sickening and warm" by the run's third lap (roughly
hour 7-8 of her race). **This plan intentionally steps carb intake down
through the day rather than holding 90g/hr throughout — holding it would be
extrapolating past what you've actually proven.**

## Fueling plan, by leg

**Swim (~1:15-1:25 estimated)**: no fueling during — impractical and
unnecessary at this duration. Pre-swim: 1 gel (~25-30g carb) ~15-20 minutes
before your start, normal hydration that morning. No fluid loss concern
here (wetsuit swim, cool water).

**T1 (~5-8 min)**: 1 gel + ~200-300ml fluid. Starts the bike fueling clock.

**Bike (~5:15-5:45hr estimated for A goal)**: **75g/hr carb** — stepped
down from your proven 90g/hr since this leg alone exceeds your tested
window. Use your glucose:fructose blend (drink mix + gels) via your
existing on-bike setup. **Fluid: ~0.9 L/hr (ESTIMATE)**. **Sodium: ~900mg/hr
(ESTIMATE, derived: 0.9L/hr × 1,000mg/L)**. The bike leg is where most of
your total-race calories should land — gut tolerance and gastric emptying
are generally better on the bike than on the run, so front-load here.

**T2 (~5 min)**: brief top-up, grab any special-needs nutrition.

**Run (~3:15-3:45hr estimated for A goal)**: **60g/hr carb** — a further
step-down from the bike rate. This is deliberate and evidence-based:
running jostles the gut more than cycling, fatigue is highest here, and the
dossier's own real-world account shows gels failing around this point.
**Plan for a hard product switch in the second half of the run** — bananas,
pretzels, cola, watermelon from aid stations, not just more gels. **Fluid:
~1.0-1.1 L/hr (ESTIMATE, slightly higher than bike — more heat
accumulation, more impact)**. **Sodium: ~1,000-1,100mg/hr (ESTIMATE)**.

**Total carbohydrate across bike + run: ~625-675g.** That's roughly 20+
gels'-worth — say this out loud now so the logistics (how much you're
actually carrying/buying) aren't a race-morning surprise.

## Pacing plan, by leg

**Swim**: no real pacing structure needed at this fitness level — the
priority is managing the panic risk already flagged in the dossier, not
speed. **Start toward the back or side of the field**, not the middle of
the mass start, to avoid the contact/chaos that makes cold-water panic
worse. Settle into rhythm before worrying about pace at all.

**Bike — flagged limit**: your FTP (240W) is 2 months stale with almost no
recent power data, so **I can't give you a real power-based pacing number
yet** — the research is clear this course rewards power-based pacing over
feel, but that requires a current, validated FTP. **Do a fresh FTP test
before the next version of this plan.** Until then, pace by effort/HR:
sustained **Z2-low Z3** (124-166bpm per your zones) for most of the ride,
saving legs for the run. In the two wind-exposed marshland sections per
loop: **hold power/effort steady, let speed drop** — chasing speed into a
headwind costs disproportionate energy for the time gained. On the two
Cat-4 climbs (~4.2% grade, ~17m each, both loops): ease back slightly
rather than pushing through — they're short, the time cost of easing off is
small, the fatigue cost of powering through isn't.

**Run**: flat course, no elevation adjustment needed. **First 1-2km
deliberately slower than goal pace** — legs need a moment to find a running
rhythm off the bike, this isn't the moment to test that. After that, settle
into goal effort by feel/HR. For the psychological weight of 4 laps passing
the finish area repeatedly: **the dossier's borrowed tactic (heart-rate-only
watch mode, lap notifications off, pace by aid-station feel) is worth
testing in a training brick before race day**, not improvising for the
first time under fatigue.

## What to do with the estimates before this plan is trustworthy

1. **Fresh FTP test** — the single highest-value missing number; unlocks
   real power-based bike pacing instead of effort/HR guessing.
2. **Sweat rate test**, separately for a long bike and a long run — they're
   estimated differently above for a reason, and a real test will show
   whether that's actually true for you.
3. **Sweat sodium test** — matters more here than anywhere else in your
   season; a wrong estimate compounds over 9-11 hours in a way it can't in
   a 3-hour marathon.
4. **Extend your gut-tolerance testing past 3 hours** in training bricks —
   this is what determines whether the bike's 75g/hr step-down is too
   conservative or exactly right.
5. **Rerun this skill** once any of the above changes — every number here
   is built to be replaced, not defended.
