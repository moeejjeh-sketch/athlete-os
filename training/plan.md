each week for context. Near-term phases (through Munich + recovery) are

## Data the plan is built on

Full analysis (6 months of Strava, 2026-01-16 to 2026-07-15, 301 activities)
is summarized here; see the chat walkthrough for the complete breakdown.

**Fitness & trend**: Running climbed from ~3.4h/25km in January to a
sustained 12-16h/week block through May-June (peak 20.7h/249km in June),
now down to ~7-9h in July from a deliberate taper. Cycling and swimming
never built the same way — **68% of the last 16 weeks had zero rides, 37.5%
had zero swims**. Longest continuous efforts: run 21.1km (May/June), ride
90.2km (January, never repeated since), swim 3.4km (May).

**Injury-risk pattern found in the data**: three week-over-week volume
spikes far past safe territory — +45% (04-20), +134% (05-04), and **+484%**
(05-25, jumping from a 2.38h week straight to 13.91h). Each followed an
unusually low week. This is a recurring habit, not a one-off: low week →
big overcorrection, rather than a controlled rebuild. The plan below is
built to not repeat that pattern.

**Chronic load**: trailing 4-week average ~9.9 hours/week. Demonstrated
*sustained* capacity: ~12-16h/week held for roughly 8 weeks straight
(May-June) — real evidence of what this athlete can absorb, not a guess.

## Race demands (researched, cited)

**Munich Marathon — 2026-10-11**: single continuous loop through Olympiapark,
Siegestor, English Garden, Marienplatz. ~120m total elevation gain, gently
rolling with mild climbs around km 38-40 — right where fatigue peaks, worth
specific prep. 100% asphalt, World Athletics certified, genuinely fast.
October weather is close to ideal: 8-14°C, ~65% humidity, rain possible but
rarely heavy. No altitude factor.
[Course profile](https://statathon.com/munich-marathon/) ·
[Race guide](https://rundida.com/marathon/munich/)

**Ironman Hamburg (full) — 2027-06-06**: swim is 2 laps of the Außenalster,
still water, no current, wetsuit legal, water ~17-22°C in similar-season
conditions. Bike is 179.6km, two loops, one of the **flattest bike courses
on the EU Ironman circuit** (~300m climbing/loop) — but it runs through the
Vier- und Marschlande marshland with **very little wind protection**, so the
limiter is wind/aero management, not climbing. Run is 4 laps around the
Alster (flat, urban). June weather: 11-20°C, moderate rain risk, ~14mph
average wind. No altitude factor.
[Bike course guide](https://www.bestbikesplit.com/event/ironman-hamburg-2026-bike-course-race-plan) ·
[Race guide](https://prommer.net/en/training/triathlon/races/ironman-hamburg-2026/)

## Strengths vs. limiters

| | Munich Marathon | Ironman Hamburg |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | Strong, well-built aerobic run base (75-92% of run time already easy — close to the 80/20 target); recent 5K 17:39 shows real speed | Swim distance *capability* already close to race demand (3.4km longest vs 3.8km needed); flat course suits current run strength |
| **Limiter** | Longest run so far is 21.1km — needs to reach ~28-32km before taper for a sub-2:48 attempt to be safe, not heroic | Bike consistency (68% zero-ride weeks) and longest ride ever (90.2km) are both far short of 180km demand; almost no usable power data to set real bike zones |

**Note (2026-07-15):** distance capability isn't the same as discipline
strength — by his own assessment, **swim is the weakest of the three
disciplines overall** (newest, started ~Oct 2025, least technique/speed
development), even though the distance numbers above look reasonable. Bike
has the bigger raw volume/consistency gap against the 180km race demand.
Both need deliberate attention; neither should be deprioritized based on
the distance-capability numbers alone.

One more limiter that shows up in the HR data directly: even in the "hard"
running months (May-June), only 0-1% of run time was genuinely hard
(Z5/187bpm+) — 22-25% sat in the "moderate" gray zone instead. Per the
polarized-training research already in `plan-my-week`, that gray zone is
the least productive place to spend training time. Quality sessions in this
plan are written to be genuinely hard, not comfortably-hard.

## Ramp discipline (applies to every phase above)

- ~10%/week volume growth, measured off **actual** prior-week hours from
  Strava, not the plan — matches the `plan-my-week` methodology.
- Before capping strictly at 10%, check the trailing 4-week average
  (acute:chronic workload ratio) — if last week was artificially low (taper,
  illness, exams), rebuilding toward the chronic average is safer than
  freezing near the low week, same logic already used for the 07-16 week.
- No repeats of the historical pattern: a very low week is never followed by
  a >30% jump. If a low week happens, the week after rebuilds gradually.
- A down week every 3-4 consecutive build weeks, sized to ~40-60% of the
  preceding week's volume.
